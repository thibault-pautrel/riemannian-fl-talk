Keeping data on the client is not enough

A client sends a submission $\Delta^{(i)}$, built from $\theta_t$ and its local iterate. That submission is a known, deterministic function of its records.

The server observes $\Delta^{(i)}$. It also knows $\theta_t$, the model and the protocol, because it chose them.

So it can guess a record and compute the submission that guess would produce.

Then it minimises the distance between the two submissions, over the guess.

Reconstruction attacks Zhu et al., 2019 and membership inference Shokri et al., 2017 are exactly this, at scale.

L'optimisation tourne vraiment. Cliquer sur "new record" pour la relancer sur un autre enregistrement. Rien ici n'est propre à SPDNet, seule compte le fait que la carte enregistrement vers soumission soit connue et différentiable.

The server follows the protocol exactly. It simply keeps everything it sees.

The guarantee has to hold for $\mathcal{V}_T$ as a whole, not for one message.

$\displaystyle \widehat{\nabla} f(x,\mathcal{B}) =\frac1B\sum_{z\in\mathcal{B}}\nabla f(x,z)$
clipped

If $\mathcal{B}'$ replaces $z$ by $z'$, then $$\bigl\lVert\widehat{\nabla}^{C} f(x,\mathcal{B}) -\widehat{\nabla}^{C} f(x,\mathcal{B}')\bigr\rVert\le\frac{2C}{B}$$ whatever the two records are.

After $\tau$ such steps the client builds $\Delta^{(i)}$ from $\theta_{t,\tau}^{(i)}$. Every message in $\mathcal{V}_T$ is noisy.

Does that stop the attacker? The next definition says how much.

Premier temps: le gradient de minibatch usuel, rien de privé. Deuxième temps: le clipping, et surtout la borne, qui est ce qui rend le bruit calibrable. Troisième temps: le bruit, et les deux flèches qui sautent d'un tirage à l'autre.

Differential privacy

$(\varepsilon,\delta)$-DP
A randomised $\mathcal{A}$ is $(\varepsilon,\delta)$-DP if, for every record-adjacent pair $D\bowtie D'$ and every measurable set $E$, $$\Pr\bigl(\mathcal{A}(D)\in E\bigr)\le e^{\varepsilon}\,\Pr\bigl(\mathcal{A}(D')\in E\bigr)+\delta .$$

Here $\mathcal{A}$: one client submission $\Delta^{(i)}$, random because the gradients behind it are.

$E$: any set of outcomes the server may test.

$\varepsilon$ caps the ratio, $\delta$ is the slack.

Target: the whole transcript $\mathcal{V}_T$.

Déplacer E à la souris, l'inégalité tient partout. Baisser le bruit: les deux nuages se séparent et on trouve une région où elle casse.

The same mechanism, on a manifold

the gradient is tangent
$\operatorname{grad} f(\theta,z)\in T_\theta\mathcal{M}$
clipping, in the metric at θ
$\displaystyle \operatorname{clip}^{\theta}_C(v) =v\,\min\bigl(1,\,C/\lVert v\rVert_\theta\bigr)$
The norm changes with $\theta$, so the ball does too, and so does $$\bigl\lVert\widehat{\operatorname{grad}}^{C} f(\theta,\mathcal{B}) -\widehat{\operatorname{grad}}^{C} f(\theta,\mathcal{B}')\bigr\rVert_\theta \le\frac{2C}{B}.$$
where does the noise live?
A Gaussian of $\mathbb{R}^{d\times p}$ cannot simply be added: the sum would leave $T_\theta\mathcal{M}$. Pick an orthonormal basis $\bigl(E_1(\theta),\dots,E_d(\theta)\bigr)$ of $T_\theta\mathcal{M}$ for $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_\theta$, and draw $\xi=\sum_{k} w_k\,E_k(\theta)$ with $w_k\overset{\text{iid}}{\sim}\mathcal{N}\bigl(0,\sigma^2(2C/B)^2\bigr)$.
privatised Riemannian gradient
$\displaystyle \widetilde{\operatorname{grad}} f(\theta,\mathcal{B}) =\widehat{\operatorname{grad}}^{C} f(\theta,\mathcal{B})+\xi \ \in T_\theta\mathcal{M}$
Point clé à faire passer en déplaçant theta: la boule de clipping change de forme, parce que la norme dépend du point. La base orthonormée aussi, il faut la recalculer à chaque theta.

What we propose compact submanifolds

$\mathcal{M}$ is a compact submanifold of $\mathbb{R}^{d\times p}$, carrying the inherited metric. Every step below is then an ambient operation followed by the same projection $P_\theta$.

gradient
$\operatorname{grad} f(\theta,z)=P_\theta\bigl(\nabla f(\theta,z)\bigr)$
clipping
$\lVert v\rVert_\theta=\lVert v\rVert$ for every $v\in T_\theta\mathcal{M}$
The inherited metric is the ambient one, so the clipping ball no longer depends on $\theta$. The Euclidean sensitivity $2C/B$ carries over unchanged.
noise
$\xi=P_\theta(Z),\qquad Z\sim\mathcal{N}\bigl(0,\sigma^2(2C/B)^2 I\bigr)$
Projecting an isotropic ambient Gaussian gives an isotropic Gaussian on $T_\theta\mathcal{M}$. No frame to build, and it coincides with the intrinsic definition of the previous slide.
then any optimiser
The updates are computed from $\widetilde{\operatorname{grad}} f(\theta,\mathcal{B})$ alone, so the choice of optimiser and of retraction is left free.
Déplacer theta: la boule verte garde la même taille, contrairement à la slide précédente. Compacité: elle garantit que la projection est bien définie et que les quantités restent bornées.

From $(\varepsilon,\delta)$ to $\varepsilon\mapsto\bigl(\varepsilon,\delta(\varepsilon)\bigr)$

A test is a set $E$: the attacker says used when the message lands in it. Two error rates follow.

Rewriting the definition with those two rates gives, for every test, $$\text{miss}\;\ge\;1-\delta-e^{\varepsilon}\,\text{false alarm} \qquad\text{and}\qquad \text{miss}\;\ge\;e^{-\varepsilon}\bigl(1-\delta-\text{false alarm}\bigr),$$ two straight lines. Their maximum is the red floor.
Les points violets sont des tests possibles. Aucun ne descend sous la courbe. Une paire ne donne qu'un plancher, la courbe les résume tous.

One number: $\mu$

The hardest direction is the line joining the two centres.

Along it, the two clouds are two Gaussians.

Their gap over the noise is $\mu$.

$\mu$-GDP: attacking is no easier than telling $\mathcal{N}(0,1)$ from $\mathcal{N}(\mu,1)$ on one draw.
Our per-step mechanism: $\mu_0=2C/(B\sigma)$.
Le lien direct avec la slide du mécanisme: 2C/B est ce qu'un enregistrement déplace, sigma est le bruit. Le rapport est mu.
