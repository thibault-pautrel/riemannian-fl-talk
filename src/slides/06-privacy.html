Keeping data on the client is not enough

A client sends a submission $\Delta^{(i)}$, built from $\theta_t$ and its local iterate. That submission is a known, deterministic function of its records.

The server observes $\Delta^{(i)}$. It also knows $\theta_t$, the model and the protocol, because it chose them.

So it can guess a record and compute the submission that guess would produce.

Then it minimises the distance between the two submissions, over the guess.

Reconstruction attacks Zhu et al., 2019 and membership inference Shokri et al., 2017 are exactly this, at scale.

L'optimisation tourne vraiment. Cliquer sur "new record" pour la relancer sur un autre enregistrement. Rien ici n'est propre à SPDNet, seule compte le fait que la carte enregistrement vers soumission soit connue et différentiable.

Threat model

The server follows the protocol exactly. It simply keeps everything it sees.

Honest, and curious.

Every round adds a line: the broadcast model, the sampled cohort, and each selected client's update.

$\mathcal{V}_T=\bigl(\{(\theta_t,S_t,\{\Delta^{(i)}\}_{i\in S_t})\}_{t=0}^{T-1},\theta_T\bigr)$

The guarantee has to hold for $\mathcal{V}_T$ as a whole, not for one message.

Insister: les données ne bougent jamais, et pourtant la pile de messages grandit. C'est elle qu'il faut protéger.

What exactly do we protect?

Two databases over the same cohort of clients.

Record-adjacent, $D\bowtie D'$: one client's dataset differs by a single substituted record.

Everything else is untouched, including the other clients.

Record level, not client level. We hide one training example, not whether a hospital took part.

$\textcolor{#009E73}{\operatorname{clip}_C}(v) =v\,\min\bigl(1,\,C/\lVert v\rVert\bigr)$, the radial projection onto the ball of radius $C$

minibatch gradient
$\displaystyle \widehat{\nabla} f(x,\mathcal{B}) =\frac1B\sum_{z\in\mathcal{B}}\nabla f(x,z)$
clipped
$\displaystyle \widehat{\nabla}^{C} f(x,\mathcal{B}) =\frac1B\sum_{z\in\mathcal{B}} \textcolor{#009E73}{\operatorname{clip}_C}\bigl(\nabla f(x,z)\bigr)$
If $\mathcal{B}'$ replaces $z$ by $z'$, then $$\bigl\lVert\widehat{\nabla}^{C} f(x,\mathcal{B}) -\widehat{\nabla}^{C} f(x,\mathcal{B}')\bigr\rVert\le\frac{2C}{B}$$ whatever the two records are.
privatised
$\displaystyle \textcolor{#6B3FA0}{\tilde{\nabla} f(x,\mathcal{B})} =\widehat{\nabla}^{C} f(x,\mathcal{B}) +\mathcal{N}\!\Bigl(0,\sigma^{2}I\Bigr)$
and so is the update
$\displaystyle \theta_{s+1}\leftarrow\theta_s-\eta\, \textcolor{#6B3FA0}{\tilde{\nabla} f(\theta_s,\mathcal{B}_s)}$
After $\tau$ such steps the client builds $\Delta^{(i)}$ from $\theta_{t,\tau}^{(i)}$. Every message in $\mathcal{V}_T$ is noisy.

Does that stop the attacker? The next definition says how much.

Premier temps: le gradient de minibatch usuel, rien de privé. Deuxième temps: le clipping, et surtout la borne, qui est ce qui rend le bruit calibrable. Troisième temps: le bruit, et les deux flèches qui sautent d'un tirage à l'autre.

Differential privacy

For every record-adjacent pair $D\bowtie D'$ and every set of outcomes $E$, $$\Pr\bigl(\mathcal{A}(D)\in E\bigr)\le e^{\varepsilon}\,\Pr\bigl(\mathcal{A}(D')\in E\bigr)+\delta .$$

Substituting one record shifts the law of what is released. Only the noise keeps the two close.

$\varepsilon$ is the slack allowed on the right-hand side: the red law is scaled by $e^{\varepsilon}$.

Take $E$ to be the outcomes where blue still sticks out. That is the set that hurts the inequality most.

On that $E$, the left-hand side still exceeds the right-hand side by the shaded area. $\delta$ is exactly what has to cover it.

Curseurs: baisser sigma écarte les deux lois et delta explose, monter sigma les superpose et delta tombe. C'est tout le compromis, sans autre machinerie.
